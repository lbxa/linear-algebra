% Correct 4 - 3/5 to 17/5 and retain the third column's second entry, 2.
\noindent\textbf{Solution.}
We simplify the matrix one column at a time. Adding a multiple of one
column to another leaves the determinant unchanged. At each step,
\(c_j\) denotes column \(j\) of the current matrix.
We work from right to left, choosing each multiple to cancel an entry
below the diagonal.

First replace \(c_3\) by \(c_3-\frac15c_4\). The third and fourth
entries of this column become
\[
  4-\frac15\cdot3=\frac{17}{5},\qquad
  1-\frac15\cdot5=0.
\]
Thus
\[
  \det M=\det\begin{bmatrix}
    2&1&0&0\\
    1&3&2&0\\
    0&1&\tfrac{17}{5}&3\\
    0&0&0&5
  \end{bmatrix}.
\]

Next replace \(c_2\) by \(c_2-\frac{5}{17}c_3\), using the updated
third column. The second and third entries become
\[
  3-\frac{5}{17}\cdot2=\frac{41}{17},\qquad
  1-\frac{5}{17}\cdot\frac{17}{5}=0.
\]
Therefore,
\[
  \det M=\det\begin{bmatrix}
    2&1&0&0\\
    1&\tfrac{41}{17}&2&0\\
    0&0&\tfrac{17}{5}&3\\
    0&0&0&5
  \end{bmatrix}.
\]

Finally replace \(c_1\) by \(c_1-\frac{17}{41}c_2\), using the
updated second column. The first and second entries become
\[
  2-\frac{17}{41}\cdot1=\frac{65}{41},\qquad
  1-\frac{17}{41}\cdot\frac{41}{17}=0.
\]
We now have an upper triangular matrix, so its determinant is the product
of its diagonal entries. Hence
\[
  \det M=\det\begin{bmatrix}
    \tfrac{65}{41}&1&0&0\\
    0&\tfrac{41}{17}&2&0\\
    0&0&\tfrac{17}{5}&3\\
    0&0&0&5
  \end{bmatrix}
  =\frac{65}{41}\cdot\frac{41}{17}\cdot\frac{17}{5}\cdot5
  =65.
\]
\qed
