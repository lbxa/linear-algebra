\section{Maps between different spaces}
\label{sec:linear-algebra-i:determinants-between-spaces}

% Source: October 8 PDF, p. 20.
For a linear map \(L:V\to W\), pullback is defined even when the domain
and codomain are different. If \(\dim V=\dim W=n\), it gives a map
between one-dimensional vector spaces,
\[
  L^*:\Lambda^n W^*\longrightarrow\Lambda^n V^*.
\]
For an endomorphism, these two spaces coincide, so the map is multiplication
by a well-defined scalar. When \(V\) and \(W\) are different, there is no
canonical identification of their top-degree form spaces, and hence no
canonical scalar for this map.

\begin{definition}
  For \(L:V\to W\) with \(\dim V=\dim W=n\), its determinant is
  the map
  \[
    \det(L)=L^*:\Lambda^n W^*\longrightarrow\Lambda^n V^*.
  \]
\end{definition}

The determinant in this setting is therefore a linear map between two
lines. It acts on a form \(A\) by the same formula
\[
  (L^*A)(v_1,\ldots,v_n)=A(L(v_1),\ldots,L(v_n)).
\]
