\subsection{The dot product}

For columns \(v,w\in\R^n\), the dot product sums the products of
corresponding coordinates. Transposing a column gives a row, so
\[
  v\cdot w=\sum_{j=1}^n v^jw^j=v^Tw=w^Tv=w\cdot v.
\]
The dot product is positive definite, symmetric, and linear in each
input. These are the properties used to define an inner product on
an abstract real vector space.

\subsection{The abstract definition}

\marginnote{Checking the properties in this order often makes proofs
  easier. Positive definiteness can rule out a candidate quickly.
  Once symmetry is established, linearity in the second argument
  implies linearity in the first. For real \(a,b,c,d\) and
  \(v_1,v_2\in V\), put \(w=cv_1+dv_2\). Then
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
      &(av_1+bv_2,w)\\
      &\quad=(w,av_1+bv_2)\\
      &\quad=a(w,v_1)+b(w,v_2)\\
      &\quad=a(v_1,w)+b(v_2,w).
    \end{aligned}
  \]
}

\begin{definition}
  An inner product on a real vector space \(V\) is a function
  \[
    B:V\times V\longrightarrow\R
  \]
  with the following properties.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item It is positive definite, so
      \[
        B(v,v)\ge0,\qquad B(v,v)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v=0.
      \]
    \item It is symmetric, so \(B(v,w)=B(w,v)\).
    \item It is bilinear. For \(a,b\in\R\) and \(u,v,w\in V\),
      \[
        \begin{aligned}
          B(au+bv,w)&=aB(u,w)+bB(v,w),\\
          B(u,av+bw)&=aB(u,v)+bB(u,w).
        \end{aligned}
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

We also write \((v,w)\) for \(B(v,w)\). In this notation,
parentheses denote an inner product when their value is a real scalar;
the context distinguishes them from an ordered pair.
In the multilinear terminology of
\autoref{sec:linear-algebra-i:antisymmetric-multilinear-functions}, an
inner product is a tensor with two inputs. Both inner products and
determinant functions are examples of multilinear functions.

\subsection{Why the real field matters}

Complex bilinearity is incompatible with positive definiteness. If a
complex bilinear function satisfied \((v,v)>0\) for a nonzero \(v\),
then
\[
  (iv,iv)=i^2(v,v)=-(v,v)<0.
\]
Thus the real definition cannot be carried over unchanged to a complex
vector space.
